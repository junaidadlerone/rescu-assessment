# RES-103 — before/after

## Repro

Cold-start on the Pixel 4 emulator. Open six different deal pages (back after each),
then tap Add to bag on the seventh.

## Before

```
[rescu 16:27:24.510] re-checking availability for deal 1
[rescu 16:27:24.514] re-checking availability for deal 2
[rescu 16:27:24.514] re-checking availability for deal 3
[rescu 16:27:24.514] re-checking availability for deal 4
[rescu 16:27:24.514] re-checking availability for deal 5
[rescu 16:27:24.515] re-checking availability for deal 6
[rescu 16:27:24.720] GET /deals/3
[rescu 16:27:24.764] GET /deals/5
[rescu 16:27:24.802] GET /deals/4
[rescu 16:27:24.832] GET /deals/1
[rescu 16:27:25.084] GET /deals/6
[rescu 16:27:25.100] GET /deals/2
```

Six previously-visited deal controllers still subscribed to `CartService.itemCount`,
each firing an availability re-check on the single cart mutation.

## After

Same repro after a hot restart. One `re-checking availability` line, one `GET /deals/6`.
Every prior visit's `Worker` is disposed with its controller.

## Root cause

`ever(rx, cb)` in GetX registers `cb` on `rx`'s stream and returns a `Worker`. The
`Worker`'s `.dispose()` is the only way to unregister. `CartService` is a permanent
`GetxService` (`Get.put(..., permanent: true)` in main.dart) — its `itemCount` stream
never closes on its own. So a `Worker` that was created but never disposed keeps its
callback subscribed for the life of the process, and keeps the `DealDetailsController`
that owned it alive with it.

If the subscription had been on one of the controller's own `Rx` fields, closing the
controller would have closed the field, cancelled the stream, and dropped the listener
without needing to touch the `Worker`. It's the cross-lifetime subscription (controller
listening to service) that makes the manual dispose mandatory.

## Fix

- Store the `Worker` returned by `ever(...)` in `_availabilityWatcher`.
- Override `onClose` and call `_availabilityWatcher?.dispose()` before `super.onClose()`.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Cache `dealRepo.fetchById` responses / dedupe requests.** Collapses six network calls
  into one but leaves the six dead controllers subscribed and in memory. Fixes the log,
  not the leak.
- **Move the subscription out to a `DealDetailsBinding` and dispose it there.** Would
  work but couples binding lifecycle to controller state, and the controller-owned
  `Worker` is the more common Flutter pattern.

## Known omission

If the current controller's own `_recheckAvailability()` future is in flight when the
route is popped, its `_quantityLeft.value = ...` runs against a closed `RxnInt`. GetX
swallows this silently in the current version. Same class of edge case as the
`didUpdateWidget` question raised in RES-102 — real, minor, out of scope for a leak
ticket. A `CancelableOperation` around the fetch would address it.
